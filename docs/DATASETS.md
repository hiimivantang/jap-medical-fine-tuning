## Leakage controls (measured, not assumed)

- JMED-LLM evaluation documents are drawn from the same public corpora as MedTxt: 107/148 MedTxt-CR articles
  and 50/72 MedTxt-RR training reports appear (near-)verbatim in the JMED-LLM evaluation sets (5-gram
  containment = 1.0). Every MedTxt document with character 5-gram containment ≥ 0.2 against **any** of the 400
  JMED-LLM evaluation documents is removed from training; the removed IDs are listed in
  `data/processed/leakage_report.json`. Case reports separate cleanly (40 kept docs all < 0.2; all removed ≥ 0.5).
- MedTxt-RR **training** reports are excluded entirely: radiology corpora contain several reports of the *same*
  case written by different radiologists, so a different report of an evaluation case would leak content even
  without copied text.
- No TNM-staging or ADE-certainty training data is used at all. The JMED-LLM RRTNM items come from the RR-TNM
  *training* split (76/108 RR-TNM train reports have containment 1.0 with JMED-LLM items) and CRADE comes from
  the MedTxt-CR ADE table of the same articles. RR-TNM validation/test reports are not verbatim copies (max
  containment 0.5–0.7, typical 0.2–0.4) but describe the same 27 lung-cancer cases read by multiple
  radiologists, so they were not used either (design choice: avoid case-level leakage). CRADE and RRTNM
  therefore measure pure transfer of the medical-reading skill, not task-specific training.
- Synthetic test notes use a held-out portion (25%) of the term inventory, and test notes with 5-gram Jaccard
  ≥ 0.6 to any training note are dropped.
- `jmedft data validate` re-checks: no evaluation prompt appears in training, no test document overlaps a
  training document above threshold, SHA-256 of every processed file.

## Pre-training contamination risk

| Benchmark | Public since | Risk | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| JGLUE (JCQA, JNLI) | 2022 (GitHub), widely mirrored, part of llm-jp-eval | **High** | Absolute scores may be inflated for all models; we only use *paired differences* (E3 − E1) for H3, where contamination affects both arms equally. |
| JMMLU-Med | 2024 | High | same treatment as JGLUE |
| JMED-LLM / MedTxt | 2022 (MedTxt), 2024 (JMED-LLM) | Medium | Qwen3 (2025) and Swallow (2026) could have seen these texts; E3 adds no exposure because overlapping documents are excluded from our training data. |
| Our synthetic test sets | generated 2026-10-19, never published | **None** (by construction) | But generator-style bias is possible — mitigated by the cross-generator (Nemotron) test set. |
